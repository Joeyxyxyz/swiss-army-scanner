Focus & signal

Position and focus with the live view, then check the fringe signal before capturing a base.

Live view

holds the rig; stops when you leave this tab, when a rig action is queued, or after 30 s with nobody watching
Live view is off.

Red pixels are saturated (≥250 counts). Saturation flattens the fringes, so lower the exposure or level until the red goes away. The flood pattern focuses the camera on scene texture; stripes or checker focus the projector.

Focus score

peak —

Variance of the Laplacian over the lit area (higher = sharper). Turn the focus ring until the bar reaches the peak.

Check signal

Projects white, black and phase fringes, then reports fringe modulation (gc_conf), valid pixels and focus. It also checks that the fringes run across the baseline.

Measure gamma

Projects a gray staircase and fits the projector's response. HDMI video mode applies degamma.

Scan assistant

Sweeps the camera exposure (and optionally the DMD duty) and recommends the setting with the best fringe modulation and no saturation. It never edits a preset. Apply fills the Setup form for you to review and save.

Fast mode: flash pattern pack

Builds a DLPC350 pattern pack (BMPs, manifest, procedure) for the hardware-triggered fast path (about 0.14 s per axis instead of about 15 s). Files only: it never touches the rig. A person switches the controller into pattern mode in the TI GUI. See docs/FLASH_PATTERN_MODE.md.

Codes

Reads QR codes and 1D barcodes with the DMD as the light source. It brackets brightness and tries red, green, blue and white light. No base or calibration needed.

Decoders need about 3 camera px per module. At the 2.7 ft bench standoff a dense QR code must be about 70 mm wide.

Grid & zones

Projects a lettered grid onto the scene: columns A, B, C… left to right and rows 1, 2, 3… top to bottom, as the camera sees them. Read the photo, then scan only the zone your part is in, for example C4 or C4:D6. An AI agent can read the same photo and zone names.

1 · Projector map

Decodes both projector axes on the current scene, so each grid cell maps to exact camera pixels. It is needed to cut a scan to a zone and to place drawn zones. The scene must be lit only by the projector (steady room light) and the rig must be fixed. Capture a new map after moving the camera, projector or focus. It takes about as long as two scans.

Virtual sensors

The DMD draws a light spot of any size, anywhere, and the camera is the receiver. Size a spot the way you would pick an industrial laser-spot sensor, aim it at a grid cell, then teach part-present or alignment sensors. A sensor is only valid at the geometry it was taught at.

3 · Part-present sensor

Photoelectric compares the returned light with the empty field: blind to a part that reflects like the background. Background suppression triangulates the spot, so dark and bright parts read the same. The spot diameter and measured scale come from step 1. A zone lights whole grid cells (photoelectric only).

4 · Alignment

Teach the part's pose (centroid, angle, extents) from a scan of a well-placed part, then check later scans against it. Positions are in mm only with a lateral calibration for this geometry; otherwise camera px, judged against the px tolerance.

Cell & 3D

Work on a stored scan: export a mesh for CAD / printing, unwrap a standing cell (cylinder fit, seen arc, dent map) and bake per-pixel surface maps. Numbers are in mm only with a matching depth calibration; otherwise they are relief px (rule 6). The rig sees an open arc of the can, never the back.

Radius is the known cell radius (used by cylinder mesh, unwrap and dents; clear it to let the fit decide).

Surface maps

Height, cavity, normal, validity and density from the scan. Add a spectral reading for albedo, a light separation for subsurface, and a golden reading for dust. Readings must be of the same scene and geometry.

Checker scan

Projects a checkerboard, finds its corners (camera px ↔ DMD mirrors) and the local stretch of each cell. Uses the rig.
